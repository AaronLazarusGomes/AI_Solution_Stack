From Exposure to Productivity Impact

How to interpret AI sensitivity scores for workforce planning and time savings

Building on the task-level exposure data

Across all 600 tasks analyzed, 86% have at least one AI capability flag. But exposure does not mean replacement. We can categorize each task by the depth of AI impact to understand what changes and how:

THREE IMPACT CATEGORIES

14%84 tasks

Human-Led

Tasks with no AI capability flags. Require judgment, leadership, relationship management, or strategic thinking.

Invest in developing these skills. They become more valuable as AI handles routine work.

12%72 tasks

AI-Augmented

Tasks touched by a single AI capability. Humans remain in the loop while AI improves speed or accuracy.

Target for near-term productivity gains. Employees work faster with AI assistance, not replacement.

74%444 tasks

AI-Transformed

Tasks intersecting 2+ AI capabilities. These face the deepest transformation as multiple AI tools converge however the human is still in the loop (this is an enhanced form of augmentation).

Requires rethinking how the task is performed. Prioritize these in job architecture reviews.

Thinking about Productivity

Reframe the conversation: Instead of “AI will save X% of time,” consider how “AI changes the nature of the work.”

Purely Human-Led tasks see limited AI time savings, but become more valuable as routine work shifts. AI-Augmented tasks offer the clearest productivity gains: faster execution with human oversight. AI-Transformed tasks require new workflows, so productivity gains come after process redesign, not immediately.

1

2

3

Implications for Workforce Planning

Development investment should shift toward these five themes as routine execution moves to AI. Roles that combine deep domain knowledge with judgment, relationship management and cross-functional influence become the scarcest capability.

Unlocking AI-Driven Workforce Efficiency

Enriching Workforce Planning

Client Need

1

A Fortune 100 Technology organization sought clear, quantitative evidence of how much workforce efficiency AI could unlock, specifically to inform forward-looking workforce planning and guide leadership decisions about where and how to scale AI across the enterprise.

Their objective was not just to understand AI’s impact on roles, but to estimate time savings, financial upside, and priority areas for investment.

Approach and Insight

2

We extended Aon’s AI Sensitivity and Workforce Impact methodology to quantify hours at risk, efficiency potential, and function-level opportunities:

  • Analyze roles: Assessed Client’s job population to identify where AI could augment or automate work.
  • Estimate time savings: Modeled capacity unlocked through AI, showing it can address 10–15% of work hours across key functions.
  • Quantify financial impact: Translated time savings into enterprise value, demonstrating efficiency and cost-avoidance benefits that can be reinvested.
  • Prioritize interventions: Identified function-specific opportunity areas to guide leadership on sequencing AI adoption for maximum impact.

Impact

3
  • Data-backed value sizing: Up to $270 million in efficiencies or cost avoidance to reinvest in growth, innovation, and talent.
  • Executive alignment: Shaped the Executive Committee’s AI roadmap—where to begin, scale next, and which functions benefit most.
  • Function-level prioritization: Targeted interventions across Engineering, Sales, Support, and Operations—sequenced action, not diffuse experimentation.
  • Evidence-based planning: Translated AI opportunities into measurable workforce capacity to strengthen planning and organization structures.
